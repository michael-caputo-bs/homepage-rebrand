Why SGPs and parlays are hard to make

Three ways a maker
loses on correlated markets.

Every book prices the same slip differently, and no one publishes a fair price for it. An RFQ gives you the legs and the stake and expects a price back in milliseconds. The market moves between quotes, and a stale number on a suspended market is a loss you booked yourself.

01 / PRICING

Naive math misprices every slip

Multiplying leg odds treats the legs as independent. They aren't. Price too generously and you get picked off. Price too cautiously and you get no fills.

02 / REFERENCE

No fair value to anchor to

Books don't agree on what a correlated slip is worth. Most desks pull a handful of books, blend them by hand and add their own vig. Every quote is anchored to whichever books happen to be open.

03 / TIMING

Stale quotes get hit

Injury news, a lineup change, a line pulled from the board. The quote you left out is the one someone takes. You need to know when to widen, and when to stop.

01 · Correlation-aware SGP pricing

See what every book
charges for correlation.

Every price is the book's own combined quote for the slip, never the legs multiplied together. When the legs move together, the price shows it.

Each book also returns correlation_pct: how much shorter its quote is than the naive product of the legs. That is the correlation it's charging, as one number you can compare across every book on the slip.

  • Every supported book priced in one POST /api/sgp request
  • Set timeout_ms per request, so a slow book never costs you the quote
  • Cross-fixture parlays via /api/parlay, with same-game groups priced by each book's own bet builder
  • Pregame markets across 12+ supported sportsbooks

02 · Market fair price

An independent fair price
for correlated markets.

The Betstamp PRO True Line gave player props a reference price that doesn't belong to any one book. Parlays and same-game parlays have never had one.

The market fair price fills that gap: one vig-free number for the slip, derived from the market's SGP quotes and returned beside the per-book prices in the same response. Quote against the market, not against whichever book happens to be open.

  • One fair price per slip, correlation included, vig stripped
  • Same request, same legs, same IDs as /api/sgp
  • Backtested the way the True Line is
  • Or bring your own leg prices, and we apply the market's correlation

03 · Volatility & off-board signal

Know when to stop quoting.

You get picked off on the quotes you leave out while the market moves. The signal watches the sources behind each leg and tells you when a market has gone off the board, and why, and when the price underneath a slip is too unstable to quote.

It returns a per-market state your auto-quoting logic can act on: quote, widen, or stop. When the market settles, the signal clears and you're back in.

  • Per-fixture, per-market volatility state, updated as legs move
  • Off-the-board status with a reason code, across the sources behind the price
  • Designed to gate SGP and parlay quoting, not just straight markets

  1. Authenticate once

    Every endpoint takes an X-API-KEY header. Entitlements set which leagues, books and products your key can reach.

  2. Resolve your legs

    Pull fixtures, players and markets from the reference endpoints. The same IDs work everywhere, so a leg you built from the markets feed prices without remapping.

  3. Price the slip

    Post the legs to /api/sgp or /api/parlay. Get each book's quote with its correlation, and the market fair price for the slip.

  4. Gate your quoting

    Read the volatility signal before you send a quote. Stable, quote. Volatile, widen or stop. Your rules, your thresholds.

REST and streaming

Snapshot markets over REST, or subscribe to the Markets Stream over Server-Sent Events for live updates. Pricing endpoints are request/response.

Unified IDs

One fixture, player and market model across sportsbooks, exchanges, prediction markets and DFS. No cross-book mapping on your side.

Limits sized to your flow

Request rate and monthly volume scale to your workflow. Quoting a million RFQs a day is a scoping conversation, not a ceiling. Every error says exactly which cap you hit.

Your clock, not ours

An RFQ waits for nobody. Set timeout_ms and the response comes back when you said it should. Books that answered are priced. Books that didn't are marked, not waited for.
